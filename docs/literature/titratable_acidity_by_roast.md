# Titratable acidity (TA), pH, acid content and water-alkalinity effects in brewed coffee by roast level — literature extraction

Purpose: numbers for a first model of "residual acidity ≈ TA of the brew − alkalinity of the brewing water" for filter/drip coffee at light, medium and dark roast.
Date of search: 2026-09-14. All numbers below are transcribed as printed in the source; no unit conversion is done inside the data tables. Proposed conversions are in section 8.

Access legend used throughout:
- FULL TEXT = read from the open full text (PDF, PMC/Europe PMC XML, or publisher HTML) and the number was seen in the raw text or table.
- SUMMARY = open full text existed but the number was read through an automated page summary, not from the raw text; re-check the table before use.
- ABSTRACT ONLY / UNVERIFIED = only the abstract was readable.
- GREY = blog, magazine, trade article; not peer reviewed.

Local copies used (scratchpad): rao2018.html + tables, rao2020.xml, fuller2017.xml, PMC11002029.txt (Anokye-Bempah 2024), diss2.pdf (Yeager 2021 dissertation), batali_dissertation.pdf (actually Liang 2023 dissertation), batali2020.pdf, laukaleja2022.pdf, iop2018.pdf.

## 1. Titratable acidity of brewed coffee by roast level

### 1.1 Prime target: Batali, Cotter, Frost, Ristenpart, Guinard (2021), "Titratable Acidity, Perceived Sourness, and Liking of Acidity in Drip Brewed Coffee", ACS Food Sci. Technol. 1(4):559–569, DOI 10.1021/acsfoodscitech.0c00078 — ABSTRACT ONLY

Status: paywalled ("Available to Purchase", $48 pay-per-view). Checked: ACS page (loaded in browser; abstract + Supporting Information description only), ResearchGate ("Request PDF"), Semantic Scholar (openAccessPdf empty), Europe PMC (not indexed), eScholarship (no postprint found; the UC Davis dissertations found on eScholarship are Yeager 2021 and Liang 2023, not Batali's), Google-type searches for "all versions".

What the abstract states (verbatim, ACS page):
- "Sour perception and acidity liking were assessed over a wide range of brew strengths and extraction yields at three different roast levels (light, medium, dark) and three different brewing temperatures (87 °C, 90 °C, and 93 °C)."
- "perceived sour intensity correlates weakly with pH, but strongly with titratable acidity."
- "our data show that titratable acidity is linearly correlated with total dissolved solids (TDS) under all conditions studied, indicating that TDS is a good proxy for titratable acidity."
- Supporting Information: "Supplemental Figure 1 of response surface maps for titratable acidity of individual roast levels (light, medium, and dark) (PDF)" — the SI itself is also behind the paywall ("You do not currently have access to this content").

No TA values, slope, endpoint pH, Agtron numbers or water recipe could be read. Everything from this paper is UNVERIFIED.

Sister-study details that almost certainly apply to the same brewing setup (same group, same Curtis G4 brewers, same coffee/year) — from Batali et al. 2020, Sci. Rep. 10:16450, "Brew temperature, at fixed brew strength and extraction, has little impact on the sensory profile of drip brew coffee", FULL TEXT (eScholarship postprint qt7200z8cg, Methods p. ~11):
- Brewer: "Curtis G4 Single 1.0 Gal brewers ... flat bottomed brew basket and disposable paper filters"; 3075 g water per brew; brew ratio varied by coffee mass; grind 770 / 928 / 1115 µm.
- Brewing water: "prepared by dissolving 1.16 g CaSO4·2H2O, 4.97 g MgSO4, 3.26 g NaHCO3, and 2.57 g KHCO3 per litre of deionized water and leaving the solution to equilibrate with ambient CO2 until a stable pH near 7 was reached, as specified by industry standards". Transcribed as printed; note that these masses per litre would give an alkalinity far above the SCA 40 ppm-as-CaCO3 target, so either the recipe is a concentrate or the text has a units slip — do not use without checking the original SI.
- Target grid: TDS 1.15–1.35 % × extraction 18–22 % = SCA "ideal" region; the 2020 paper used one medium roast (Loring S35, 11:29 total, first crack 9:00, 2:30 development).
- TA in the group's other papers: "0.1 M NaOH ... until reaching a pH of 8.20 ± 0.01 ... mL NaOH/50 mL coffee" (Liang 2023 dissertation p. 181) and "pH of 8.24 ± 0.06 ... mL NaOH/50 mL coffee" (Yeager 2021 dissertation p. 66). The 2021 ACS paper very likely used the same endpoint (~8.2) — UNVERIFIED.

### 1.2 Rao, Fuller & Grim (2020), "Physiochemical Characteristics of Hot and Cold Brew Coffee Chemistry: The Effects of Roast Level and Brewing Temperature on Compound Extraction", Foods 9(7):902, DOI 10.3390/foods9070902, PMC7404565 — FULL TEXT (Europe PMC XML)

Method (Section 2): green organic Colombian arabica; bench-top HotTop roaster, default profile, beans ejected at final bean temperatures 194 °C (R1, "light"), 203 °C (R2, "medium"), 209 °C (R3, "dark"); no Agtron. Grind sieved to 500–2000 µm. Brew: 20 g coffee + 200 mL deionised water (ratio 1:10). Hot brew: 100 °C water, French-press style, pressed at 6 min. Cold brew: 20–25 °C, 7 h. Statements: "The acidity of all coffee beverages decreased as degree of roast increased"; "As degree of roast increased, the TTA of all coffees decreased (Figure 1)".

### 1.5 Anokye-Bempah, Styczynski, Fernandes, Gervay-Hague, Ristenpart, Donis-González (2024), "The effect of roast profiles on the dynamics of titratable acidity during coffee roasting", Sci. Rep. 14:8237, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-57256-y, PMC11002029 — FULL TEXT (Europe PMC XML)

Method: Probat P5, 4 kg batches, seven 16-min roast profiles (start 215 ± 8 °C, end 237 ± 2 °C), samples pulled each minute; three origins (Uganda Sipi Falls washed "USF", El Salvador Ataco honey "ELS", Sumatra washed "SUM"). Brew: 3.3 g ground coffee + 60 g purified water (Nestlé Pure Life, pH 7.46) at 94 °C, 18:1 water:coffee, 30 min covered immersion to TDS plateau, filtered through Hario V60 paper. TA: automatic titrator, "40 mL aliquot ... 0.1 mol/L NaOH ... endpoint pH equal to 8.2 ... TA is expressed as mL of NaOH per 40 mL sample". n = 3 roast replicates per profile; 663 samples total.

Values (text, Results):
- Green coffee: TA 2.2 ± 0.3 mL NaOH/40 mL, pH 5.9 ± 0.1.
- Peak TA "during first crack", all profiles and origins: 4.9 ± 0.2 mL NaOH/40 mL (USF 4.8 ± 0.1; ELS 4.9 ± 0.3; SUM 5.1 ± 0.2).
- At onset of second crack: 2.5 ± 0.2 (FS), 2.7 ± 0.1 (EF), 2.8 ± 0.2 (PR) mL NaOH/40 mL — "decreased to almost exactly the initial TA value".
- End of 16-min roast (post second crack, 237 °C): 1.2 ± 0.1 (FS, NRoR, MD profiles) to 1.7 ± 0.2 (EM, PR, SS, EF) mL NaOH/40 mL.
- Cited: Wang & Lim 2012 maximum TA 5.2 mL NaOH/40 mL.
Caveat: roast level is a position along the roast (first crack ≈ lightest drinkable roast; second crack ≈ dark), not Agtron; extraction is to plateau (higher than drip); TDS not printed in text.

## 2. Chlorogenic acids and organic acids by roast level

### 2.1 Yeager, Batali, Guinard, Ristenpart (2023), "Acids in coffee: A review of sensory measurements and meta-analysis of chemical composition", Crit. Rev. Food Sci. Nutr. 63(8):1010–1036, DOI 10.1080/10408398.2021.1957767 — publisher page blocked (Cloudflare), but the identical meta-analysis is Chapter 2 of the Yeager 2021 dissertation (FULL TEXT, eScholarship qt0w62z1n1). Dataset: Dryad DOI 10.25338/B8C91C (files Acids_in_Coffee_-OAs.csv 36 kB, Acids_in_Coffee_-CGAs.csv 1.46 MB, References.csv, ReadMe.xlsx) — download blocked by an anti-bot check; NOT OBTAINED.

Methodology worth recording (dissertation pp. 17–20): 121 publications (paper version: 129 publications, 8,634 data points), all concentrations converted to mg/kg dry coffee; roast bins assigned from (1) self-description, (2) water loss 11–13 % = light, 14–16 % = medium, 17–20 % = dark, or organic roast loss 2–4 / 4.1–5.5 / 5.6–7 %, (3) bean L* 30 / 25 / 20 = light / medium / dark. Brew-based mg/L data were converted to mg/kg using the recipe "assuming full extraction".
Results are given only as box plots (Figs. 2.2–2.8), no numeric table. Trends stated:
- Arabica organic acids: "citric and malic acid decrease with roasting ... especially when comparing green coffee to medium roasted coffee"; "Acetic acid increases proportionally with roast level; dark roasts contain the highest amounts"; lactic "relatively constant"; quinic data too sparse in roasted arabica; "Arabica coffee tends to have lower amounts of OAs, which decrease with roast level, plateauing at a medium roast."
- Chlorogenic acids: "total CQA, 5-CQA, total diCQA, and total FQA ... highest in the green coffee and decreases progressively with roasting"; 3-CQA and 4-CQA slightly higher in light roast than green, then decrease; "initial CGA concentrations on average are near 60 to 70 g/kg and roasting to medium or dark decreases them sharply".
- Robusta: 2–5× total organic acids of arabica (paper abstract), dominated by acetic and formic acid.
Numeric per-roast medians are only recoverable from the Dryad CSV or by digitising the box plots.

## 4. Slope of TA versus TDS

- Batali et al. 2021 (ACS, ABSTRACT ONLY): "titratable acidity is linearly correlated with total dissolved solids (TDS) under all conditions studied". No equation, slope or intercept readable.
- Santanatoglia et al. 2024 (UNVERIFIED snippet): Pearson r between TA and TDS % = 0.9133 (light), 0.7172 (medium), 0.8859 (dark).
- Liang 2023 diss. p. 182 (FULL TEXT): TA increased with brew time (i.e., with TDS) while pH stayed stable.
- Yeager 2021 Table 3.2 (FULL TEXT) gives TA at a fixed 2.0 % TDS, which is one anchor point per roast for a TA-vs-TDS line (see section 8 for the proportional-scaling assumption).
- Anokye-Bempah 2024 (FULL TEXT) gives TA at maximum extraction for an 18:1 ratio; TDS not printed.
No source with a published TA = a·TDS + b equation was obtained. The slope must be assumed (e.g., proportional through the origin) or fitted from the two anchor recipes above.

## 5. Effect of brewing-water alkalinity on brew TA or pH at fixed coffee and recipe

No peer-reviewed measurement of brew TA or pH versus controlled bicarbonate alkalinity was obtained.

Peer-reviewed, partial:
- Fibrianto et al. 2018, IOP Conf. Ser. Earth Environ. Sci. 102:012095 (FULL TEXT, open): 11 commercial waters, pour-over 3.5 g coffee / 50 mL at 92–96 °C, Indonesian arabica/robusta blend (roast not specified). Table 2 water: Ca 0–6.8 mg/L, Mg 0–1.2 mg/L, Na 0.4–167.6 mg/L, "CaCO3" 0–21.9 mg/L (alkalinity not measured; water pH 5.8–8.25). Table 3 brewed ground-coffee pH by water: coffee A 5.55–6.1, coffee B 5.5–6.1 (highest with tap water code 5, lowest with low-mineral waters 8–11); instant coffees 4.8–5.1. ## 8. Proposed unit conversions (assumptions written out; not applied inside the tables above)

Definitions: 1 mL of 0.1 N (= 0.1 M for NaOH) NaOH contains 0.1 mmol OH− = 0.1 meq.
- mL of 0.1 N NaOH per 100 mL brew: 1 mL ⇒ 0.1 meq / 0.1 L = 1.0 meq/L.
- mL of 0.1 N NaOH per 50 mL brew (UC Davis papers): 1 mL ⇒ 0.1 meq / 0.05 L = 2.0 meq/L.
- mL of 0.1 N NaOH per 40 mL brew (Rao papers, Anokye-Bempah): 1 mL ⇒ 0.1 meq / 0.04 L = 2.5 meq/L.
- mg/L as citric acid (triprotic, M = 192.12 g/mol): 1 mg/L ⇒ 3 × 1/192.12 = 0.0156 meq/L (only valid to a pH-8.2 endpoint where all three carboxyls are titrated).
- Water alkalinity: 1 meq/L = 50.0 mg/L as CaCO3 = 61.0 mg/L HCO3− = 1 mmol/L HCO3−. SCA target 40 ppm as CaCO3 ⇒ 0.80 meq/L; 4 °dH alkalinity (Rune 2023 water) ⇒ 4 × 17.85 = 71 mg/L as CaCO3 ⇒ 1.43 meq/L.

Endpoint harmonisation: TA to pH 8 ÷ TA to pH 6 = 1.84–2.06 for hot brews (Rao & Fuller 2018, Table 2, six coffees). A TA to pH 6.5 (Rao 2020) therefore sits well below a TA to pH 8.2 for the same brew; do not mix endpoints in one bin without scaling.

Strength harmonisation: the ACS abstract says TA is linear in TDS. If the line is assumed to pass through the origin (untested assumption; the intercept is not available), TA at drip strength = TA_measured × (TDS_target / TDS_measured).

Water: the titration already includes any bicarbonate consumed during brewing, so "TA − alkalinity" should be applied only to TA values measured with zero- or known-alkalinity water. Rao 2020 used DI water (alkalinity 0). Yeager 2021 and Anokye-Bempah 2024 used Nestlé Pure Life purified water (alkalinity not stated in either paper). Rao 2018 / Fuller 2017 used tap water of 174 mg/L hardness with unreported alkalinity.

Worked conversions (derived, for reference only):
- Rao 2020 hot brew, endpoint 6.5, TDS 1.96 / 2.12 / 2.23 %: 4.82 / 3.55 / 2.06 mL per 40 mL ⇒ 12.1 / 8.9 / 5.2 meq/L as measured; scaled proportionally to 1.25 % TDS ⇒ 7.7 / 5.2 / 2.9 meq/L.
- Yeager 2021, 92 °C, endpoint 8.24, 2.0 % TDS, mean of three origins: light (10.03 + 10.10 + 9.25)/3 = 9.79; medium (8.40 + 9.43 + 8.67)/3 = 8.83; dark (6.20 + 6.40 + 5.60)/3 = 6.07 mL per 50 mL ⇒ 19.6 / 17.7 / 12.1 meq/L at 2.0 % TDS; scaled proportionally to 1.25 % TDS ⇒ 12.2 / 11.0 / 7.6 meq/L.
- Anokye-Bempah 2024, endpoint 8.2, 18:1 ratio, full extraction: peak at first crack 4.9 mL per 40 mL ⇒ 12.3 meq/L; at second crack 2.5–2.8 ⇒ 6.3–7.0 meq/L; end of roast 1.2–1.7 ⇒ 3.0–4.3 meq/L (TDS not stated; 18:1 with plateau extraction is somewhat stronger than a 20 %-yield drip at the same ratio).
- Rao 2018 hot brew light roasts, endpoint 8, 1:10 ratio: 6.40–7.85 mL per 40 mL ⇒ 16.0–19.6 meq/L (TDS not reported; roughly 2 % expected at 1:10).

## 9. Best available TA per roast bin for a first model

Recipe the bins apply to: hot-water brew, TA titrated to pH ≈ 8.2, expressed at a drip strength of 1.25 % TDS by proportional scaling from the measured strength (assumption: TA ∝ TDS through the origin, consistent with the ACS abstract's "linearly correlated" but with an unknown intercept). Water: low/unknown alkalinity (Nestlé Pure Life) for the primary source, so treat these as "TA before subtracting alkalinity" only approximately; for a strict zero-alkalinity baseline use the Rao 2020 DI-water values with the endpoint caveat.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light | Agtron Gourmet whole bean 56–59 (ground 67–68), weight loss 12–14 %, end bean temp 204–208 °C (Yeager 2021); ≈ just after first crack (Anokye-Bempah 2024) | ≈ 12 | 10–14 | Yeager 2021 Table 3.2, 92 °C, three origins 9.25–10.10 mL/50 mL at ~2 % TDS ⇒ 11.6–12.6 meq/L scaled; Anokye-Bempah peak 12.3 meq/L at 18:1 full extraction; Rao 2018 light roasts to pH 8 at 1:10 give 16–20 meq/L unscaled (≈10–12 if ~2 % TDS is assumed) |

Roast-to-roast ratios (dark / light): Yeager 2021 0.62; Rao 2020 hot 0.43 (endpoint 6.5, unscaled) and 0.38 (scaled); Anokye-Bempah second crack / first crack 0.51–0.57.

Data sufficiency: adequate for a first light/medium/dark split with ±20 % uncertainty on the central values; not adequate for (a) the TA-vs-TDS intercept, (b) drip-specific (percolation, ~20 % extraction) TA — all readable sources are immersion brews, (c) TA in water of known non-zero alkalinity, and (d) any measured alkalinity→TA or alkalinity→pH slope. The single paper that would settle (a) and (b), Batali et al. 2021 (ACS), is paywalled; purchasing it ($48) or requesting it from the authors is the fastest way to close the gap. For (d), no published controlled experiment was found; the model's subtraction term rests on stoichiometry (1 meq/L bicarbonate neutralises 1 meq/L of titratable acid at a pH-8.2 endpoint), not on a measured coffee dataset.
